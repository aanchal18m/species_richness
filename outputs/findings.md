## 9. The argument, in bullets

- Question: can environmental variables predict winter bird richness in poorly
  sampled India well enough to identify biodiversity patterns?
- Broad pattern: moderately, yes -- C1 passes (rho 0.631 under 100 km
  blocking) but only under the revised block rule; C3 passes widely (92.1%).
- Hotspots: no -- C2 fails at every validation level, including random CV
  (0.595); about half of true hotspots are missed. Predictions compress
  the extremes, so the richest cells are under-predicted.
- The RF earns its complexity: beats linear and null under every scheme (spatial
  rho 0.631 vs 0.479 vs undefined; R^2
  0.41 vs 0.21).
- Validation decline (S1): rho 0.716 random -> 0.631
  100 km -> 0.585 400 km; C2 0.595 -> 0.524 -> 0.487.
- Ecology (S3): built-up land strongly negative (urbanisation), open water positive
  (winter waterbirds), rainfall seasonality positive; mid-elevation hump visible
  but not confirmed (section 10).
- "The limit is the predictors, not the data" -- three lines, with their strength:
  1. Quantity: halving the training data barely matters (rho 0.614 at 50% vs
     0.631 at 100%); more data of the same kind is not the bottleneck.
     Strong. (It still falls to 0.528 at 10%.)
  2. Coverage of environmental space: 92.1% of poorly sampled cells resemble the
     training cells; failure is not extrapolation. Strong.
  3. Unexplained spatial structure: RF out-of-fold residuals autocorrelate at short
     range (0.39 within 10 km, 0.20 at 10-25 km, < 0.1 by 25-50 km).
     MODERATE: consistent with missing local drivers, but equally with shared local
     sampling effects (same observers in neighbouring cells).
  4. Noise in the response is not the limit (split-half reliability,
     section 7b): full-depth reliability 0.954, so a perfect model could
     reach rho ~0.98 (achieved 0.631) and hotspot recall ~0.88
     (achieved 0.524). The 0.70 C2 bar was attainable in principle. Strong for
     sampling noise; optimistic because halves share observers and sites, so error
     shared across a cell's observers remains unmeasured.
  - Net: data quantity, environmental coverage and sampling noise are each ruled
    out as the main limit; what remains is the predictor set (and any error shared
    across a cell's observers, which no test here can see).
- Where the model may not be used: high Himalaya (>= 3,000 m: 22%-36% inside),
  Andaman & Nicobar (15 of 65 inside), Thar/Kutch and NE hills fringes.

## 10. Limitations (all)

- **Block-size rule revised after the first result.** First run: response-based
  400 km blocks, RF rho 0.576 (fail; first-run value, recorded in CLAUDE.md). Revised to residual-based 100 km blocks
  (Roberts et al. 2017): rho 0.631 (pass); the original rule now gives
  0.585 (fail). The C1 verdict depends on this post-hoc choice; report both rows.
- **RF grid widened after the first run** (max_features 0.2 added; tuning had hit the
  grid corner in all 10 folds).
- **Effort leaks slightly:** out-of-fold residual vs T rho 0.10
  (CI 0.06-0.15). Direction ambiguous: effort passing the coverage
  standardisation, or observers choosing places richer than their environment predicts.
- **Mid-elevation hump not confirmed by the pre-set test** (decile bins merged
  927-5,428 m); visible in fixed bands; only 163 training cells above 1,500 m;
  elevation shares signal with temperature predictors (elev_mean VIF 13.5).
- **Proposal 6.5 Level 3 not run.** The stress test thins training cells (data
  quantity); the proposal's version thins sampling units within cells (shallow
  response). Different question.
- **N sensitivity covers C1 and C2 only** (section 7c): random CV, 400 km blocks,
  SHAP and AOA were run at N = 3 only; predictors were not re-screened per N.
- **bio12 clipped** at 6,553.5 mm in 245 cells (104 more than half clipped); bio12
  and bio16 both dropped by VIF; bio13 carries wet-season rainfall.
- **Training pool concentrated in the south:** 36.5% of training-eligible cells lie
  south of 15N vs 10.5% of all grid cells.
- **GBIF route:** eBird records carry no eventID or completeness flag; units are
  (cell, point, day) pooling observers (rule C), which merges independent same-day
  hotspot visits and slightly understates coverage. eBird Basic Dataset not used.
- **Coverage standardisation guard:** T >= 20 units; the guard simulation is
  optimistic for sparse cells (units from rich cells hold more species).
- **CHELSA artefacts:** Andaman & Nicobar temperature range is marine (65 cells,
  excluded from training); coastal diurnal range attenuated; bio3/bio4 rescaled and
  bio16/bio18 unwrapped (verified by identities).
- **Islands lost from the grid:** all of Lakshadweep, small Andaman/Nicobar and
  Sundarbans islands (391 km^2); 22,750 km^2 of land outside kept cells.
- **Temporal mismatch:** climate 1981-2010, land cover 2021, records 2015/16-2023/24;
  effort grew >5x over the period.
- **AOA weights** use in-sample permutation importance (approximating CAST's OOB
  importance); AOA and spatial CV not validated against R/CAST (R not installed).
- **Inside the AOA is not "accurate"**: accuracy there is what C1/C2 measured.
- **Predicted extremes compressed:** predictions 35-164 vs observed
  12-262 species.
- **Copernicus DEM is a surface model**; open-pit mines give elevation minima of -93 m.
- **C2 level by assumption** (same as C1); not separately decided.
- **Reliability is optimistic** (shared observers and sites between halves); the
  ceilings bound sampling noise only.

## Route decision and scope cuts (decided 2026-10-05)

- **Occurrence data come from GBIF only.** The eBird Basic Dataset will not arrive
  in time. It is not pending; do not design around it or leave hooks for it.
  The proposal's complete-checklist and effort filters (section 6.1) cannot be
  applied on this route; state that limitation in the report.
- **Cut:** IUCN threatened-species extension; 5 km sensitivity run; summer
  replication. Do not start them.
- **Andaman & Nicobar cells (65) are excluded from training but kept as
  prediction targets** (decided at Part 3 review). CHELSA's temperature-range
  values there are marine, not terrestrial; as the only cells in that region of
  predictor space a tree model would fit them exactly and learn a false
  relationship. Carry a `train_eligible` boolean in the model table; never drop
  the rows. Expect the area of applicability to mask them in Part 7 and report
  that as a worked example. Flag source: `data/interim/cell_flags.parquet`.
- **Deduplication is same-observer only** (decided 2026-10-06): clean.py's
  duplicate key includes recordedBy. Without it, different observers at one
  eBird hotspot on one day collapsed into one record -- 9.8M of 23.6M records
  deleted, 92% cross-observer.
- **GBIF sampling unit = (cell, point, day), pooling observers** -- rule C
  (decided 2026-10-06). A shared group checklist is one unit, not k identical
  ones; independent same-day visits to one hotspot are merged too, so coverage
  is slightly understated -- the recoverable direction. Rule B (observer-keyed
  units) overstates coverage in exactly the busy cells that become training data.
- **C1 is judged under spatial blocking** (decided 2026-10-06). The proposal's
  section 3 criteria table governs; section 6.5's Level 3 (simulated sparse
  sampling) is a reported stress test, not the bar.
- **Spatial CV block size = range of the RESIDUAL autocorrelation** of the linear
  model fitted to all training-eligible cells (Roberts et al. 2017): first
  correlogram band below 0.1, rounded up to 50 km. DISCLOSE IN THE WRITEUP: this
  rule was revised after an initial run (2026-10-06). The first run used the
  correlogram of the RESPONSE (-> 400 km blocks; RF spatial rho 0.576). Revised
  because richness is autocorrelated largely because the environment is, and
  explaining that is the model's job, not the CV's. The 400 km response-based
  blocking stays in cv_results as a sensitivity row.
  CONSEQUENCE, also to disclose: the revision moves C1 across its bar. RF pooled
  rho = 0.631 at the residual-based 100 km blocks (passes 0.60; fold minimum
  0.541) vs 0.585 at the response-based 400 km blocks (fails). The revision was
  made after the first result (0.576, fail) was known, so the verdict depends on a
  post-hoc analytic choice; report both rows side by side.
- **elev_mean is protected in VIF screening** (decided 2026-10-06): when it has
  the largest VIF the next-largest predictor is dropped. Elevation is central to
  the ecological reading and was otherwise represented only by elev_sd.
- **RF grid widened after the initial run** (disclose): tuning picked the corner
  (max_features 0.33, min_samples_leaf 1) in all 10 outer folds, so
  max_features 0.2 was added. Grid: max_features {0.2, 0.33, 0.66} x
  min_samples_leaf {1, 5, 20}.
- **If VIF screening keeps only one of bio12/bio16, keep bio16.** bio12 is clipped
  at 6553.5 mm in the Western Ghats, a likely richness peak; bio16 is clean after
  the unwrap. Record this reason wherever the VIF result is reported.

## Decisions that are FIXED — do not re-derive or quietly change

1. **Response variable is coverage-standardised rarefied richness, never a raw
   species count.** Raw counts measure richness multiplied by survey effort. This
   is the single most important decision in the project.
2. **Grid is equal-area** (Albers conic, standard parallels 12.5N / 32.5N). A
   degree-based grid carries a 25% area gradient from 6N to 37N in India, running
   along the same axis as the ecological signal.
3. **Season is fixed to the winter window (Nov–Feb).** India's migrant influx means
   annual richness confounds residency with migration.
4. **The model is a Random Forest, judged against a null and a linear baseline.**
   The brief asks for interpretation, not algorithmic novelty. Do not propose deep
   learning. *Amended 2026-10-06: XGBoost dropped.* The brief prioritises
   interpretation, and the RF already beats both baselines under every CV scheme
   (Part 5), so a second boosted ensemble would add complexity without a question
   it answers.
5. **Validation runs at three levels** — random k-fold, spatially blocked, and
   simulated sparse sampling — and all three are reported. The decline between them
   is a result, not an embarrassment.
6. **The success criteria are pre-registered and must not move:**
   - C1 Spearman rho >= 0.60 between predicted and withheld observed richness,
     under spatially blocked CV (see decisions below)
   - C2 top-quintile hotspot recall >= 0.70
   - C3 >= 0.50 of poorly sampled cells inside the area of applicability

   Compute them once. Report the achieved values whether or not they clear the bar.
   **A failing verdict is a valid and complete answer to the assigned question.**
   Never tune thresholds after seeing results.
7. **Checklist completeness is approximated by a minimum-species-per-unit
   threshold N, and N is a sensitivity parameter tested at 1, 3, 5 and 10** —
   never a single chosen value. *Status 2026-10-06: Gate 1 reported at all four;
   the model was built at N = 3 (team choice at Gate 1) and C1/C2 were rerun at
   N = 1, 5, 10 (outputs/tables/n_sensitivity.csv): rho 0.623 / 0.631 / 0.644 /
   0.646, C2 0.527 / 0.524 / 0.522 / 0.497 -- both verdicts unchanged at every N.
   Random CV, 400 km blocks, SHAP and AOA were run at N = 3 only.* GBIF carries no completeness flag (trap 6), so
   any one N would be an unverifiable guess; the results across all four values
   are reported.

## Traps already discovered — do not reintroduce

1. **Coverage formula uses two different falling-factorial bases** — numerator falls
   from `T - Y_i`, denominator from `T - 1`. Collapsing them to one base makes
   coverage return exactly 1.0 for every cell and silently disables the whole
   correction. This bug was found and fixed; do not "simplify" it back.
2. **Cells with very few records report coverage 1.0 spuriously** because there are
   no singletons. A minimum-record guard is required alongside any coverage
   threshold, or near-empty cells enter the training set as perfectly inventoried.
3. **A spatial join reports "no match" as NaN; our code reports None.** `nan != None`,
   so comparing them naively reads as a mismatch when both agree.
4. **ESA WorldCover at 10 m over India is tens of billions of pixels.** Use the 100 m
   product or aggregate in Earth Engine. Do not tile-download the 10 m globally.
5. **GBIF SQL aggregate downloads destroy sampling-unit information.** A previous
   download grouped by species x cell and lost `recordedBy`/`eventDate`/`eventID`,
   which the rarefaction method needs. If using SQL, keep a visit count via
   `COUNT(DISTINCT CONCAT(recordedby,'|',eventdate))`.
6. **eBird records on GBIF carry `eventID = null` and no "all species reported"
   flag**, and 99.3% of Indian bird records on GBIF are eBird. Sampling units are
   rebuilt from observer + date + cell, and checklist completeness is unknowable
   (hence fixed decision 7). The `incidence.py` docstring's claim that eBird data
   on GBIF carries a checklist eventID is wrong for this dataset.
7. **`coordinateUncertaintyInMeters` is null for eBird records**, so a "drop above
   10 km" filter passes almost everything. Report any such share both as a
   fraction of all records and of records that report the field.
8. **Calendar-year filtering splits winters.** Nov–Dec belong to the following
   year's winter. Keep 2015/16 to 2023/24 — nine complete winters — and drop the
   orphaned Jan–Feb 2015 and Nov–Dec 2024. (The download is the 2015–2024
   calendar superset; the trim happens in cleaning.)
9. **GBIF SQL downloads may need enabling per account.** A 4xx from
   `python src/gbif_download.py request` means that, not a bug.
10. **exactextract applies a GeoTIFF's own scale/offset.** Applying them again
    turned CHELSA bio1 into about -270 degC. Pass raw files; never rescale its output.
11. **CHELSA bio3 and bio4 are not in the units their tags imply.** bio3 is stored
    already /10 (x100 to get %); bio4 is SD x100 (x0.01 to get degC). Both are
    checked against physical identities in `tests/test_env_covariates.py`.
12. **CHELSA uint16 precipitation overflows in the Western Ghats and Meghalaya.**
    bio12 clips at 6553.5 mm (lost: use `bio12_saturated_frac`); bio16/bio18
    wrap and are unwrapped by `fix-chelsa`. Always extract via `chelsa_source()`.
13. **WorldCover's internal overviews are majority-resampled.** They understate
    fragmented classes (grassland -60% in a test window). Aggregate from 10 m.
14. **A GBIF SQL download can finish SUCCEEDED with 0 records and still mint a
    DOI.** Request 0011506-260928105237408 did exactly that (DOI 10.15468/dl.g5uxhg,
    never cite it), although GBIF's validator accepted the query. Always check
    `totalRecords` before fetching or citing: `fetch` enforces this
    (`check_nonempty`) and also refuses counts more than 5% outside the
    pre-request search-API estimate (`check_record_count`). GBIF cannot validate
    predicate downloads at all (HTTP 422), so that count check is the only guard
    on the predicate route.
15. **GBIF's SQL layer uses alphanumeric taxon keys** -- Aves is classkey `'V2'`,
    not 212, and specieskey looks like `'59PK8'`; GBIF's own UI writes
    `ARRAYS_OVERLAP(taxonkeys, ARRAY('V2'))`. A hand-written `classkey = 212`
    therefore matches nothing: this, not permissions and not nulls, is why SQL
    request 0011506 was empty (diagnostic 0011565: COUNT = 0 for
    `classkey = 212 AND countrycode = 'IN'`, against 61.5M in the search API).
    GBIF's published SQL schema (`/occurrence/download/describe/sql`) still
    declares `classkey`/`specieskey` as INT and lists no `taxonkeys`, and the
    validator accepts `= 212` by wrapping it in `CAST(... AS INTEGER)` -- neither
    warns. SIMPLE_CSV predicate downloads carry the numeric backbone keys
    (speciesKey 2493598 = *Lonchura punctulata*). `build_richness.to_numeric`
    raises on any non-numeric key rather than coercing it to NaN, where
    clean.py's species-rank filter would drop it unseen.
